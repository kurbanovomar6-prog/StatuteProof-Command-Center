## 4. Outreach Positioning

### What to say

> "We monitor official regulators and extract compliance-relevant updates for fintech, payment and legal teams in undercovered markets — Central Asia, Caucasus, Turkey and the GCC."

> "You tell us your markets and industry. We configure a source profile, monitor official regulators, and deliver structured AI compliance briefs and Telegram alerts to your team."

> "Coverage is honest: we show you exactly which sources we monitor, their health status, and what we cannot yet reach."

### What not to say

- "Global regulatory monitoring" — not credible at this stage.
- "Complete coverage" — no regulatory monitoring platform has complete coverage.
- "We cover all regulations in [country]" — never say this.
- "AI guarantees compliance" — AI generates briefs; legal review is required.
- "We monitor 50 countries" — do not overclaim.

---

## 5. Why Not to Sell as "Global RegTech"

Claiming global coverage creates problems:

1. **Credibility** — Enterprise buyers will immediately ask for a source list and health proof. If coverage is weak in their markets, trust is lost.
2. **Expectation mismatch** — "Global" implies completeness. RegRadar is strong in specific markets, not everywhere.
3. **Competition** — Global positioning means competing with Thomson Reuters, LexisNexis and Refinitiv — impossible at this stage.
4. **Support burden** — Claiming global coverage creates support requests for countries RegRadar cannot yet serve.

---

## 6. Why "Undercovered Markets" Is the Right Positioning

The undercovered markets angle is the strongest unique selling point because:

1. **No strong competitor exists** in Central Asia and Caucasus regulatory monitoring.
2. **Pain is real** — Compliance teams operating in Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Georgia and Azerbaijan have no automated monitoring tool for these jurisdictions.
3. **Language advantage** — RegRadar handles Kazakh, Uzbek, Azerbaijani, Turkish and other regional languages while delivering English briefs.
4. **International buyer demand** — International law firms, fintech companies and payment providers operating in these markets need this.
5. **Sales conversation is easier** — You do not need to compete on features; you compete on coverage of a region no one else covers well.

## 8. Suggested First Outreach Sequence

### Phase 1 — Warm reach, zero paid outreach

1. Post on LinkedIn about RegRadar's UAE / Turkey / Kazakhstan monitoring.
2. Share a sample compliance brief publicly.
3. Reach out personally to 5–10 compliance professionals in the target markets.
4. Offer a free 2-week pilot to 2–3 early clients in exchange for feedback.

### Phase 2 — Focused cold outreach (Apollo or manual)

Target job titles:
- Chief Compliance Officer
- Head of Regulatory Affairs
- VP/Director of Legal and Compliance
- Managing Partner (legal firm with ME/CIS practice)
- Compliance Manager

Target companies:
- Fintech companies with UAE or Turkey operations
- Payment companies with Central Asia operations
- International legal firms with ME/CIS regulatory practice
- Crypto exchanges or VASPs registered in UAE

### Phase 3 — Scale after first 3–5 paying clients

Only scale outreach after:
- At least 3 paying clients
- Documented case study or testimonial
- Source coverage proven to clients' satisfaction
- No major product reliability issues

## 10. What to Avoid

- Do not claim customers or logos you do not have.
- Do not inflate coverage numbers.
- Do not claim AI "guarantees" compliance.
- Do not overcommit on adapter or new-source delivery timelines.
- Do not sell to Belarus or Iran without legal review.
- Do not position as Russian-language or Russia-focused product.
